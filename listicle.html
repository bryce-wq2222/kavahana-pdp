NOTES FROM THE BAR

6 things about kava I
learned behind the bar

Years of drinking it, years of pouring it for other people, and the 6 things I still end up explaining.

By Hana Wilen

Co-founder, Kavahana · September 3, 2026 · 4 minute read

The first time someone asked me what kava was, I was standing at a folding table at a farmers market. Whisk in my hand.

I found it years before we opened anything, looking for something to do at night that wasn’t a glass of wine. Alcohol does take the edge off. Then it bills you for it at 7am.

Kava didn’t do that. What convinced me was a Sunday, about a month in. I’d been out the night before, and I was up at 8 making coffee like it was a Tuesday.

So Neil and I hauled that table around LA. Markets, pop-ups, 1 cup at a time for anyone curious enough to try. There are 2 bars now and I’m still behind a counter most days.

1

It’s a talking drink

This is the part of the job I’ve never gotten over. A guy came in on a first date last winter and ordered for both of them. Then he spent 10 minutes explaining kava to a woman who already knew what it was.

About 20 minutes in, something in his face loosened. Relaxed, smiley, a little surprised.

When it lands it’s 10 to 20 minutes in, and it runs an hour or 2 after that. That’s usually the moment people look at the cup instead of at me.

He stopped explaining and asked her a question. They were still there an hour later.

2

The lip thing

First timers usually stop mid-sentence and touch their bottom lip. Then they look at me like I did something to them.

I didn’t. The kava plant is in the peppercorn family, and that’s the whole explanation. Lips and tongue go tingly seconds after the first sip.

Then they touch it again, to check. Then usually a third time.

People ask if there’s kratom in it. There isn’t. It’s the juice of that root, cold-pressed and dried, and that’s the whole list.

3

I didn’t feel much the first time either

The thing I hear most from people who’ve already tried kava somewhere else is that they felt nothing.

My first cup was on a friend’s kitchen floor and I sat there waiting for a light to come on. Nothing came on. I went home mildly annoyed and fairly sure I’d been sold a nice story about a plant.

I went back the next week because he kept asking. That time it landed. Kava works backwards from most things, and many people find they need less over time, not more.

So that’s what I tell the ones who felt nothing. Many people do 2 to 3 servings their first few times. I tell them to come back once before they decide.

4

Nobody drinks it straight

A woman at the counter once asked for it plain, the way you’d order an espresso. I poured her a small cup.

It’s earthy and peppery, closer to a strong matcha than to anything sweet. She got half of it down and asked what everyone else does.

Everyone else mixes it. Juice, coconut milk, whatever’s cold. Cold coconut water is what I hand people first, and never warm, which brings out the bitterness.

People have enjoyed it in the South Pacific and Hawaii for over 3,000 years. In all that time I’ve never heard of anyone making it sweet.

She made a face on the first sip, then ordered a second, mixed. If someone tells you the taste is the best part, they’re selling you something.

5

The root is older than the company

A guy at the bar asked me what we do to the root. I told him almost nothing, and he looked disappointed.

It’s a fair question with a boring answer. The plant does the work.

What I handed him was whole root, no stems and no leaves. It grew 5 to 7 years in volcanic soil in Vanuatu before anybody pulled it.

We’re 3 years into this. The root in the cup I handed him started growing before there was a Kavahana.

I didn’t tell him that. It would have sounded like a line, and it’s the only genuinely impressive thing about us.

6

It takes 15 seconds and a whisk

People always assume there’s more to it. They picture a pot, a strainer and an entire evening.

I scoop it into something cold and non-alcoholic, whisk until it foams, and pour it over ice. It’s 15 seconds, start to finish.

I watched someone whisk their first cup at the bar and look genuinely annoyed when it was done. He’d spent the walk in telling his friend it was going to be a whole production.

He kept whisking another 20 seconds anyway, just in case. It did not get any more done.

If you want to try it, the Starter Kit is what I’d hand you across the counter.
