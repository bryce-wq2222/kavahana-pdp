THE SUBSCRIPTION OFFER

01

How the offer works

Value-framed discount

The % off is computed against the full value of the box's contents, not the kava's MSRP. Big number up front, premium protected underneath.

the kava stays full price forever
Itemized contents

Every item gets its own dollar value, listed line by line like a receipt. What's included justifies the deal, not a markdown.

cups $23.98 · sticks $17.50 · whisk $14.99 · recipe guide always free
Subscription-gated

The free items only exist on the subscription. One-time purchase stays at full price with nothing free. It is the anchor, not the product.

one-time starter kit: $94.99 · no gifts
02

The offer, both sizes

Discovery shows subscribers skewing to the larger size, so the offer now runs on both. Both sizes include the ritual kit free, the two shell cups, the whisk and Hana's recipe guide. The 5 stick packs are the exclusive reason to size up, which is also what logistics can support right now. Stick packs then return free on renewals 1 and 2 as the retention hook. The larger tier is the one to push. Both are sold in servings rather than ounces, because a 45 serving order ships as more than one bag and ounce labelling has already read as a downgrade in the review corpus.

What the stick packs are actually buying. Holding them out of the 22 serving box lifts its first-order contribution to +$16.76, because it no longer carries $5.25 of stick COGS, and narrows the headline gap between the tiers to 37 against 32. Putting 5 free sticks back on renewals 1 and 2 costs $5.25 each time, so those two renewals contribute $23.20 and $56.75 rather than $28.45 and $62.00. That is a deliberate trade: roughly $10.50 of margin spent per subscriber, placed exactly at the 77 day window where they leave. Giving the sticks at renewal actually widens the gap between the tiers, because the same $5.25 lands much harder on the smaller basket. The 45 serving subscriber is now worth more than eight times the 22, $108.56 of lifetime profit at two renewals against $13.16, and still all but pays for its own acquisition on the first order.
